Data warehouse - is a subject oriented, integrated, time variant and non volatile collection of data to support managmenetns decision making process.

Subject oriented - A data warehouse is organized around a major subjects, such as customer, supplier, product and sales.
Integrated- A data warehouse is usually constructed by integrating multiple heterogeneous sources.
Time variant - data are stored to provide info from a historical perspective.
Nonvolatile - meaning that data in the db is never overwritten or deleted, but retained for future reporting.


Data warehousing - is the integrated, architected and periodic copying data from various heterogeneous sources, both inside and outside the enterprise into a centralised repo optimised for reporting and analytical processing.

📊 What is a Fact?
Definition: A fact is a numeric, measurable value that represents a business event or transaction.

Examples:

Sales revenue (₹5000)

Quantity sold (10 units)

Hours worked (8 hours)

Characteristics:

Stored in fact tables

Usually contain foreign keys linking to dimensions

Often aggregated (sum, average, count) for analysis

📊 What is a Dimension?
Definition: A dimension provides descriptive context for facts.

Examples:

Customer (Name, Age, Location)

Product (Category, Brand, Price)

Time (Date, Month, Year)

Characteristics:

Stored in dimension tables

Contain attributes that describe the “who, what, when, where” of a fact

Enable slicing and dicing of data for reporting

🔍 How to Identify a Fact Table
Contains measurements or metrics: Numbers you can aggregate (sales amount, quantity, revenue, hours worked).

Granularity: Each row represents a business event or transaction (e.g., one sale, one shipment).

Foreign keys: Usually references multiple dimension tables (customer_id, product_id, date_id).

Few descriptive columns: Mostly numeric values and keys.

Example:

Columns: Date_ID, Customer_ID, Product_ID, Sales_Amount, Quantity_Sold.

🔍 How to Identify a Dimension Table
Contains descriptive attributes: Textual/contextual data (customer name, product category, region).

Primary key: A single unique identifier (Customer_ID, Product_ID).

No aggregations: Values are descriptive, not numeric measures.

Example:

Columns: Customer_ID, Customer_Name, Age, City.

🏗️ How to Recognize the Schema
Star Schema

One central fact table surrounded by multiple dimension tables.

Dimension tables are denormalized (contain rich descriptive attributes).

Easy for BI tools and fast queries.

Snowflake Schema

Similar to star, but dimension tables are normalized into multiple related tables.

Example: A “Product” dimension split into “Product” and “Category” tables.

Saves storage, but queries are more complex.

Galaxy / Fact Constellation Schema

Multiple fact tables share dimension tables.

Useful when analyzing different business processes (sales, inventory, shipping) together.
